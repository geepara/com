title = "Feedback Is Only Worth Giving To Agents"
date = "2026-10-22"
description = "Feedback that used to teach people goes to agents, and if the agent learns everything once, people lose their reason to learn."
tags = ["ai", "learning", "work"]
+++

Feedback used to be how people got better at their jobs. Someone with more experience corrected your work, and once you understood the correction you did better the next time.

Once an agent does the work, the feedback goes to the agent. You can see this on pull requests. A reviewer leaves comments and the author hands them to a coding agent, which makes the fixes, and the author doesn't read them.

You can teach the agent yourself, and people are doing it. Teams are building software factories, where agents write the code and the people around them improve the instructions the agents follow. Ramp's motto for Ramp Glass is "learn everything once." You write a lesson into an agent's skills and no one on the team has to learn it again. That works, and it will spread to other industries as agents take on more of the work, but it creates a bigger problem.

If the agent learns everything once, you have no reason to learn it yourself. Learning used to pay off. You struggled to understand something until you could do it on your own, and that made you more valuable. If the agent can do it, you get nothing for the struggle and you lose the incentive to learn. Work gets less fun, since figuring things out was a big part of what made it satisfying. Learning gets harder, because the agent does the work that used to teach you.

Agents change what you're worth to an organization. If agents do the work, you're useful to the company as far as you can improve the agents' skills, and each time you teach the agent something you know, the organization needs you a little less. Once you've deposited all of your knowledge, the one job left for you is directing the work, and the organization might not need a person for that.
