> People pick college and take on debt because it's the only path with proven outcomes.

<br>

College is expensive. The system that puts people in debt for a degree is broken, which is why [we need a new education system](/a-new-school/). People take on the debt anyway because a degree is the one path with a track record they trust. You can point to someone who went, finished, and got the job. The outcome is proven, so the risk feels known.

Bootcamps, online courses, apprenticeships, and teaching yourself can cost far less, but nobody could say what they actually led to, so they were hard to choose. A cheap path with no track record is a guess, and people don't bet a career on a guess. They pay for the path where the end is already visible, and they borrow to do it.

From January to September 2023 I worked on [Youni](https://useyouni.com) to try to measure those outcomes, so each path had a real return you could compare to the others and to a degree. If you could see that a $2,000 path gets you to the same job as a $200,000 degree, you would take the $2,000 path and skip the debt.

Youni was my proposed solution to the student debt crisis, and the real innovation was measuring the return on education outside of college. Once a cheaper path has a proven outcome, people can choose it instead of the debt.
